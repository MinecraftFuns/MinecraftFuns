## Reading the Prompt and Establishing the Thesis, `the Soul of the Essay`

* Source material
* Task
* Topic

**Theme** is not the same as **title**.  
**Theme** is not the same as **thesis/intent**.  

### How to Read the Task

* Prompts
* Requirements

#### Directly Related to `How to Read the Material`

> Choose two or three keywords from the material to present the China you know.  
> Base your thesis on **two or three** of the given sentences, and cite them appropriately.  

#### Directly Related to `the Direction of the Essay's Thesis`

> Advocating that everyone "**love labor**, starting with **myself**."  
> Each generation has its own opportunities and missions, its own challenges. **You** grow and travel alongside China in the new century, chasing and realizing dreams together with China's **new era**.  

#### Directly Related to `Genre and Format`

* Form (no need to overdo it)  
* Practical genres just need to be reflected  

##### Genre

> Combining the material content... write a **speech**  
> ...a **letter** to a family member  

Simply **reflecting the scenario** is enough

##### Title

> Using "How I See the College Entrance Exam" or "My College Entrance Exam" as a **subtitle**, write an essay  
> Using "The Resilience of Civilization" as the **title**, write an **argumentative essay**  

#### ▲ Directly Related to `Time, Space, and Identity`

> Write a speech addressed to **students at your school**  
> On **September 30, 2049**, write a National Day greeting letter to a "**centennial figure of merit in China**"  
> Imagine it sealed in a "time capsule" to be opened in **2035**, for **the 18-year-olds** of that time to read  

#### Standard Requirements

> Requirements: engage with the material, choose a good angle, settle on a thesis, make the genre clear, and give the piece its own title; do not write to a formula, do not plagiarize; do not reveal personal information; at least 800 characters.  
> Choose one of the two topics below and respond as required. At least 700 characters.  

### How to Read the Material

> "Rough thesis-forming"  
> Touching on the material  

#### Specific-Event Material

> **Find → List → Transform**

##### First, `Find`

1. Find the subjects, look at the event (note primary vs. secondary)
1. Find the attitude, look at the stance

> During "World War II," to improve fighter aircraft protection, British and American military authorities surveyed the distribution of bullet holes on planes that survived combat, and decided to reinforce wherever the holes were most numerous. However, the statistician **Abraham Wald** insisted otherwise, pointing out that more attention should be paid to areas with fewer bullet holes, because planes hit hard in those areas rarely made it back, and that data had been overlooked. It turned out **Wald** was *right*.  
> After several years of experimentation, **Xiaoyu** built on traditional craft to develop a new kind of flower tea and obtained a patent for it. But soon after mass production began, the market was flooded with counterfeit, low-quality knockoffs. **Xiaoyu** realized that rather than watch this newly emerging industry decline so quickly, it would be better to lead the effort to regulate the market first. So she made the production process public and led the drafting of a local standard, which the relevant local government authorities then issued and promoted. These efforts gradually paid off, the new flower tea industry grew larger and larger, and **Xiaoyu**, focusing her energy on leading her team in continuous innovation, ultimately *became a widely respected leader in prosperity-building*.

##### Second, `List`

List out phrases and sentences: play down the specifics, output the viewpoint

##### Third, `Transform`

* Turn words into sentences
* Turn simple sentences into compound sentences
* Turn the negative into the positive

#### Non-Specific-Event Material

1. Find the subjects, look at the direction
1. Turn words into sentences, simple sentences into compound sentences, negative into positive

Facing opposing viewpoints

1. Look at the task requirements first
1. Either is acceptable (pick one / write comprehensively), `keep the right outlook`

#### Fully Abstract Material

> **Extract the keywords (distinguish primary from secondary)**  
> *Each thing has its own nature*; water at its blandest, salt at its most flavorful. Water added to water is still water; salt added to salt is still salt. Sour, sweet, bitter, spicy, salty: the *five flavors blend*, coexisting and giving rise to one another, producing a hundred varied tastes. So it is with things, so it is with affairs, and so it is with people.  

Center on the core, `use the material as a springboard for your own point`

#### How to Handle Special Forms of Material

* Look at the task requirements first
* Three methods (may overlap): `(1) similarities-and-differences method` / `(2) split-and-combine method` / `(3) thread method`

`Nod` (quick lead-in, quick lead-out)  
`Split and correspond` (break apart and match up)  

##### Multiple Pieces of Material

##### Chart-Type Material

Convert the elements of the image (subjects / events) into words

## Structural Thinking Methods, `the Skeleton of the Essay`

### Three-Part Structural Thinking Method

* Core: **logic**

Common three-part structures: `parallel` / `progressive`

#### Using `Why` as the Thread

> *Main thesis* **Labor creates value, labor brings happiness (still to be refined)**  
> *Sub-thesis 1* Labor helps individuals realize their achievements and value, bringing every laborer joy and happiness from the heart  
> *Sub-thesis 2* Labor drives the development and progress of society, bringing every labor-loving nation passion and hope  
> *Sub-thesis 3* Labor is the cornerstone by which humanity creates material and spiritual civilization, giving humanity the strength and warmth to endure  

#### Using `What to Do` as the Thread

> *Main thesis* **Stay true to yourself, create a wonderful life (still to be refined)**  
> *Sub-thesis 1* Staying true to yourself means constantly striving to learn and improve, recognizing yourself clearly and correctly  
> *Sub-thesis 2* Staying true to yourself means not changing your original intent when facing temptation, and standing firm and moving forward when facing difficulty  
> *Sub-thesis 3* Staying true to yourself does not mean rejecting all outside voices, but absorbing them clearly and using them for your own purposes  

#### Using `the Material Itself` as the Thread (not recommended / used to *play it safe* / used for *nested structures*)

* What is it
* Why
* What to do

> *Main thesis* **Building a community with a shared future for humanity through a spirit of integration (still to be refined)**  
> *Sub-thesis 1* (What is it?) The spirit of integration is a spirit of harmonious coexistence, drawing on each other's strengths, and coexisting and giving rise to one another  
> *Sub-thesis 2* (Why?) Only through integration can humanity become more united, and only through integration can humanity become stronger  
> *Sub-thesis 3* (What to do?) Integration is not a simple stitching-together, but a kind of mutual growth and mutual achievement built on deep mutual understanding and empathy  

#### Using `Time` as the Thread

Past → present → future

> *Main thesis* **Proud of past achievements and glory, striving for the future and tomorrow**  
> *Sub-thesis 1* At 18, looking back on the 18 years I have lived through, the great achievements created by the Chinese people fill me with deep pride  
> *Sub-thesis 2* At 18, I am willing to advance together with the times, carrying forward the spirit of unity, striving, diligence, and courage passed down by our forebears, and contributing my own light and heat  
> *Sub-thesis 3* At this moment when you are 18, how do you view your tomorrow? On the day socialist modernization is basically achieved, what opportunities and mission will you have?  

### Two-Part Structural Thinking Method

* Core: **contrast + unity**

★ `for / against`  
`past / present`  
`internal / external` (attribution: internal factors ↔ external factors)  
`similarity / difference`  
★ `theory / reality`  
▲ `whole / individual`  
▲ `greatness / ordinariness` (divinity ↔ humanity)  

### Four-Part Structural Thinking Method (suitable for `narrative essays`)

Time slices, with setup, development, turn, and conclusion  
An 800-character essay typically writes four `scenes`  

### One-Part Structural Thinking Method

* Refining / optimizing the essay's overall thesis or **argument**

Common approaches: `the questioning method` / `falsification (pre-set assumptions)`
